# Crop Reference: Basmati Rice, Wheat, Mungbean

Pilot district: Sheikhupura, Punjab, Pakistan. Compiled 15 November 2026.

This file is a research summary, not independently verified data. It is the structured
counterpart to `data/processed/crop_profiles.json`, which a Python backend can load
directly. Do not treat single-trial or single-source figures as district-wide fact -
see the "Evidence level" and "Uncertainties" subsections for each crop.

## Tags

- **[PW]** Punjab-wide information
- **[SKP]** Sheikhupura-specific evidence
- **(A)** derived or assessed, not stated directly by a source
- **(U)** unverified or not found

KSK = Rice Research Institute, Kala Shah Kaku (Sheikhupura district).
CRS = Punjab Crop Reporting Service. UCC = Upper Chenab Canal.

## 1. Basmati rice

**Growing season** - Kharif (summer). [PW] Kharif crops are planted roughly April-July
and mostly harvested September-November.

**Sowing / harvest windows**
- [PW] Nursery should not be sown before about 20 May. Transplanting should ideally
  finish by mid-July for grain quality.
- [PW] Early-maturing basmati (Basmati 385, Super Basmati, Basmati 2000) matures in the
  first week of November; older types in the third week.
- [SKP] KSK trial: Super Basmati transplanted 1 July, harvested before 18 November.
- (U) No primary department source found for basmati-specific nursery/transplant windows.

**Duration** - Roughly 3.5-4.5 months in the field, derived from the dates above (A).
CRS lists an "average sowing days" figure of 104 for Sheikhupura basmati, but the column
definition is unclear (U).

**Water requirements** - Highest of the three crops, but no Punjab-specific mm figures
are available (U - do not invent values). [PW] About 30% of rice water use is for
puddling and transplanting; direct seeding saves an estimated 15-20%. A modelling study
for the UCC command area projects 13-18 irrigations for rice vs 2-5 for wheat under
future climate scenarios - this is a projection, not a measured baseline.

**Local suitability (Sheikhupura)**
- [SKP] CRS 2024-25: 587,000 acres of rice, of which ~570,000 (~97%) is basmati.
- [SKP] KSK soil: clay loam, pH 8.2, organic matter 0.67%.
- [PW] The rice-wheat zone is Pakistan's main fine-basmati producer.
- [PW] Water tables are declining in rice-growing districts.

**Evidence level** - Mixed. Punjab-wide agronomic guidance is reasonably well sourced;
Sheikhupura-specific evidence rests on one KSK trial plus CRS area statistics. Duration
and nursery/transplant windows are derived, not directly measured locally.

**Uncertainties / limitations**
- No primary-source basmati-specific sowing/transplant calendar for Sheikhupura.
- Water use in mm is unavailable; irrigation-count projections are model output, not
  observed data.
- CRS rice area (587,000 acres) is inconsistent with an earlier figure of 484,000 acres
  for 2020 - unresolved discrepancy, check CRS final estimates.

## 2. Wheat

**Growing season** - Rabi (winter). [PW] Planted October-December, harvested April to
mid-June.

**Sowing / harvest windows**
- [PW] Punjab Agriculture (Extension) department (Nov 2026) urges finishing irrigated
  sowing by 20 November.
- [PW] A department-leaflet copy (secondary/aggregator source) gives optimum sowing
  ~1-25 November depending on zone, with late sowing by 15 December.
- [PW] In practice, most sowing in the rice zone now falls late November-December,
  because rice is harvested late.
- [PW] Harvest runs mid-April to end of May in the plains.
- [SKP] KSK trial: sown 16 November, harvested 3rd week of April.

**Duration** - About 5 months in the KSK trial (16 Nov - 3rd week April) (A). Shorter or
longer depending on actual sowing date (A).

**Water requirements** - Moderate. No Punjab-specific mm figures available (U). [PW]
Usually 3-4 irrigations: the first 20-25 days after sowing (30-40 days in rice areas),
then at booting and milking. In the UCC command area the canal runs only in summer, so
wheat depends on marginal-quality groundwater.

**Local suitability (Sheikhupura)**
- [SKP] CRS 2024-25: 561,000 acres of wheat. An earlier CRS-based report put the figure
  at 495,000 acres in 2020.
- [PW] Late sowing is a recurring problem in the rice zone because rice is harvested late,
  which compresses the wheat window and can depress yield.

**Evidence level** - Punjab-wide sowing guidance comes from a mix of a primary department
statement (Nov 2026) and a secondary leaflet copy of unclear provenance. Sheikhupura-specific
evidence is CRS area statistics plus one KSK trial.

**Uncertainties / limitations**
- The "optimum sowing window" leaflet is an aggregator copy, not a verified primary
  department document.
- Water use in mm is unavailable.
- Area figures for 2020 vs 2024-25 differ; not reconciled.

## 3. Mungbean

**Growing season** - Spring and Kharif pulse, plus a short catch crop between wheat and
rice. [PW] Spring sowing 15 February-15 March; Kharif sowing June-July. The Pulses
Research Institute (AARI) promotes spring sowing and mung as a catch crop in rice-wheat
systems.

**Sowing / harvest windows**
- [SKP] KSK trial: sown in the 4th week of April (a week after wheat harvest), pods
  picked at the end of June.
- [PW] Dates otherwise follow the season row above (spring or Kharif sowing).

**Duration**
- Tested Pakistani genotypes reach 90% maturity in 56-91 days.
- NIAB lines: ~80 days spring, 90-95 days summer.
- [SKP] 65-70 days in the KSK trial.

**Water requirements** - Lowest of the three; short duration means less irrigation is
needed overall. No Punjab-specific mm figures available (U). [PW] Typically 3-4
irrigations, the first ~21 days after sowing, then roughly 15-day intervals.

**Local suitability (Sheikhupura)**
- [PW] Mungbean leads Kharif pulses in Punjab generally.
- [SKP] The 2024-25 CRS district profile has no moong area entry for Sheikhupura - area
  is zero or unreported (U).
- No farmer-adoption data found for Sheikhupura (U).

**Evidence level** - Weak at district level. Duration figures come from genotype trials
(not necessarily Sheikhupura), and the only local field evidence is a single 2007-08 KSK
rotation trial.

**Uncertainties / limitations**
- CRS has no recorded moong area for Sheikhupura; can't confirm it is actually grown at
  any scale in the district today.
- No adoption data.
- Water use in mm unavailable.

**Sources**
- AARI Pulses Research Institute, Kharif 2018: https://aari.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/APRW%20Kharif%202018.pdf
- AARI Pulses Research Institute, Kharif 2016: https://aari.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/APRW%20Kharif%202016.pdf
- Dawn, mungbean: https://www.dawn.com/news/242480/mungbean-a-rich-source-of-protein

- Ali et al. 2012, J. Anim. Plant Sci. 22(1) (KSK rotation trial): https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/2012-japs-103.pdf

## Scenarios

### Baseline: rice -> wheat

[SKP] The dominant pattern in Sheikhupura. CRS 2024-25 shows 587,000 acres of rice and
561,000 acres of wheat on 768,064 cultivated acres. A 2018 irrigation study (Muridke
tehsil) lists rice, wheat, sugarcane and fodder as the major crops. The KSK trial authors
call rice-wheat the "commonly practiced pattern" in this zone.

Caveats: fodder alone covers 265,000 acres, so not every field follows rice-wheat;
district totals cannot show field-level rotations (U); rice area estimates disagree
across sources (484,000 acres in 2020 vs 587,000 in CRS 2024-25).

### Alternative: rice -> wheat -> mungbean

Plausible as an added catch crop, but **unproven at farm scale** and conditional on the
gap between wheat harvest and rice transplanting.

**Evidence for**
- [SKP] A 2007-08 KSK trial of rice-wheat-mungbean found: mungbean grain 0.78 t/ha;
  paddy 3.57 t/ha vs 3.34 t/ha without mungbean; wheat 2.63 t/ha vs 2.59 t/ha; cost-benefit
  ratio 1:1.78 vs 1:1.56. The paddy gain is reported as significant; the wheat
  difference is not. One season, one site.
- [PW] A 55-70 day mungbean variety was judged feasible in the pre-rice niche at NIAB
  Faisalabad (not Sheikhupura), with a reported 21% yield increase in the following wheat.

**Constraints**
- **Window**: KSK reports ~11 weeks of fallow after an April wheat harvest, against
  65-70 days needed for mungbean. If wheat is sown late and harvested around 20 May,
  only ~4-6 weeks remain before a 20 June-1 July rice transplant (A) - likely too short.
  The window is therefore a function of actual wheat harvest date, not a fixed quantity.
- **Irrigation**: the April-June gap likely needs tubewell water where canals are
  summer-only (UCC); the Joyanwala distributary is non-perennial; canal opening dates
  are (U).
- **Adoption**: no farmer-adoption data found for Sheikhupura (U).

**Modeling guidance**: treat mungbean insertion as a scenario *conditional on* the
wheat-harvest-to-rice-transplant interval, not as a fixed, always-available alternative.
See `data/processed/crop_profiles.json` -> `scenarios.alternative.condition`.

## Known gaps (apply across all three crops)

- **Basmati duration and windows**: no primary department source for transplant-to-harvest
  days or basmati-specific nursery/transplant windows; both are derived.
- **Water in mm**: no reliable Punjab-specific values for any of the three crops. A
  Multan CROPWAT paper is internally inconsistent (its abstract gives rice 996.4 mm,
  while its discussion assigns the same figure to other crops), so it is not used here.
  UCC irrigation counts are model projections, not measurements.
- **Wheat sowing guidance**: the stated "optimum window" relies partly on an aggregator
  leaflet copy, not a primary department document.
- **Sheikhupura-specific gaps**: per-field rotation data, mungbean adoption, and
  Sheikhupura sowing dates beyond the single KSK trial are all unavailable.
- **Area figures**: rice and wheat acreage disagree between the 2020 source and CRS
  2024-25; unresolved.
